Claim Autopsy
1. Isolate the Claim

The statement contains two factual subclaims and one implied comparison:

Mercury is closer to the Sun than Venus.
Venus has a higher surface temperature than Mercury.
Implied: distance from the Sun alone does not determine a planet’s surface temperature.

The claim does not explicitly explain why Venus is hotter, although it invites that question.

2. Dissect It
Explicit claims
Mercury is the planet closest to the Sun.
Venus is hotter than Mercury.
Implied claim
Being closer to the Sun does not necessarily make a planet hotter at its surface.
Required assumption
“Hotter” refers to surface temperature—most naturally the mean global surface temperature—not temperature high in the atmosphere, inside the planet, or at a single moment and location.
Possible ambiguity

“Hotter” could mean either:

higher average surface temperature, or
higher maximum recorded or estimated surface temperature.

The statement is clearly correct under the first meaning. NASA’s published figures indicate it is also correct under the second: Venus’s roughly uniform surface temperature is about 464–467°C, while Mercury’s daytime highs reach approximately 430°C.

3. Evidence Standard
Subclaim	Evidence needed
Mercury is closer to the Sun	Reliable orbital measurements or authoritative planetary data
Venus is hotter on average	Comparable global mean surface-temperature estimates
Venus can be hotter despite receiving less sunlight	Measurements of atmospheric composition and pressure, supported by established radiative-physics models
Venus’s atmosphere causes the difference	Evidence that its atmosphere strongly restricts outgoing thermal radiation, contrasted with Mercury’s negligible atmosphere

Spacecraft measurements and planetary models validated against those observations constitute strong evidence. A diagram, textbook assertion, or calculation based only on distance would be merely suggestive unless atmospheric effects were included.

4. Examine the Evidence
Distance from the Sun

NASA gives Mercury’s average distance from the Sun as approximately 58 million kilometers, compared with approximately 108 million kilometers for Venus. Mercury is therefore substantially closer.
NASA: Mercury facts · NASA: Temperatures Across Our Solar System

Status: Established

Average surface temperatures

NASA lists the mean surface temperatures as:

Mercury: 167°C (333°F)
Venus: 464°C (867°F)

Venus is therefore about 297°C hotter on average under these published estimates.
NASA: Solar System Temperatures

Status: Established

Maximum surface temperatures

Mercury can reach approximately 430°C (800°F) during its daytime, but can fall to approximately −180°C (−290°F) at night. Venus’s surface remains around 465°C, making even its ordinary surface temperature higher than Mercury’s reported daytime maximum.
NASA: Mercury facts · NASA: Venus facts

Status: Established, subject to normal variation and rounding in planetary temperature estimates.

Why proximity does not decide the result

Mercury receives more solar energy because it is closer to the Sun, but it possesses only an extremely tenuous exosphere—not a substantial atmosphere capable of efficiently redistributing and retaining heat. Consequently, it experiences enormous day-night temperature swings.

Venus has an extremely dense, predominantly carbon-dioxide atmosphere and a surface pressure roughly 90 times Earth’s. Incoming solar energy heats the planet, while the atmosphere strongly absorbs outgoing infrared radiation. This greenhouse effect produces its extreme surface temperature and distributes heat much more uniformly around the planet.
NASA: Why Is Venus So Hot? · NASA: Venus facts

Status: Established as the principal physical explanation.

5. Autopsy Findings

The claim contains no meaningful cherry-picking or unsupported leap. Its only notable weakness is the undefined word “hotter.” Planetary temperatures vary by location, altitude, time, and measurement convention.

Nevertheless, the usual interpretation—mean surface temperature—is clear and strongly supported. Even interpreting “hotter” as maximum surface temperature does not overturn the comparison using NASA’s reported figures.

The wording “even though” might encourage the mistaken idea that distance should be the sole determinant of temperature. In reality, planetary temperature also depends on:

atmospheric mass and composition,
reflectivity,
rotation and heat redistribution,
absorption and emission of radiation.
6. Evidence Versus Inference
Established: Mercury is closer to the Sun than Venus.
Established: Venus has a substantially higher mean surface temperature.
Established: Venus’s dense atmosphere produces an enormous greenhouse effect.
Established: Mercury’s lack of a substantial atmosphere contributes to extreme temperature swings.
Inferred from the phrasing: The statement is intended to illustrate that solar distance alone does not determine surface temperature.
7. Steelman

The strongest formulation is:

Although Mercury receives more sunlight because it orbits closer to the Sun, Venus has the higher mean surface temperature because its massive carbon-dioxide atmosphere creates an exceptionally strong greenhouse effect, while Mercury lacks a substantial atmosphere.

That formulation is scientifically sound.

Autopsy Report

Original Claim:
Venus is hotter than Mercury despite Mercury being closer to the Sun.

Subclaim Findings:

Mercury is closer to the Sun — ESTABLISHED. Its average orbital distance is about 58 million km, versus Venus’s 108 million km.
Venus is hotter on average — ESTABLISHED. Its mean surface temperature is about 464°C, versus Mercury’s 167°C.
Venus is hotter because of atmospheric effects — ESTABLISHED. Its dense carbon-dioxide atmosphere strongly traps outgoing heat.
Proximity alone determines planetary temperature — CONTRADICTED. Atmosphere, reflectivity, and heat redistribution also matter.

What Survived:
The complete factual comparison. Venus is the hottest planet, while Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun.

What Didn’t:
Nothing essential. The only defect is that “hotter” is not explicitly defined as surface temperature.

What’s Still Unknown:
Nothing important to the central claim. Exact temperatures vary somewhat by location and by the averaging method used.

Verdict: CONFIRMED

Confidence: High

Authoritative planetary data directly support both parts of the claim, and established atmospheric physics explains the seemingly counterintuitive result.
